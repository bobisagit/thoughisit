Our Story

The Wagner family left Hamburg aboard the vessel Wilhelmsburg in May 1855, arriving after a 107-day passage at Hobart Town, Van Diemen’s Land. Michael and Catherine Wagner and their four children made their way up the east coast to settle at Great Swanport — the town later known as Swansea.

By 1860 the family had purchased a ten-acre allotment and used the abundant stone on the land to build a cottage, its fences and its stables. That cottage still stands at the heart of the estate today, listed on the National Trust Register of Significant Historical Buildings.

Restored for guests in 1975 by local farmers Dianne and Malcolm Sinclair — who added Hollyhock and Primrose over the following twenty years, and raised the Studio from the ruins of the old stables — the estate is today owned and personally run by Kerry and Michael, who welcome you to enjoy its tranquillity and history.

Things to Do

Wagner’s sits at the heart of Tasmania’s east coast, halfway along the Great Eastern Drive — national parks, conservation beaches, cellar doors and some of the country’s finest seafood are all within easy reach.

A busy day of travel earns a log fire and a spa. Here is how our guests fill their days.

~50 min drive

Freycinet National Park & Wineglass Bay

A peninsula of pink granite mountains and native forest edged by crystal-blue bays. Bushwalk through protected parkland, scale the Hazards, and photograph the famous curve of Wineglass Bay.

parks.tas.gov.au
Departs Coles Bay

Wineglass Bay Cruises

Step aboard for an unforgettable four-hour cruise along the Freycinet Peninsula, from the gentle shores of Coles Bay to one of the world’s most beautiful beaches, with lunch of fine Tasmanian produce.

pennicottjourneys.com.au

Maria Island Cruises

Explore Maria Island’s coastline — remote beaches, giant sea caves and the largest seal colony on Tasmania’s east coast — then walk the World Heritage-listed convict settlement of Darlington.

Great Oyster Bay is one of Tasmania’s best vantage points for humpback and southern right whales on their seasonal migrations — watch from the shore, or join a cruise for a closer look.
